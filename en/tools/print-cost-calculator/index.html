What this tool does

It calculates material cost from the filament weight and the price per kilogram. The most accurate weight comes from sliced G-code or a .gcode.3mf; you can also bring it over from the G-code analyzer or type it in.

For an STL, OBJ or 3MF model that is not sliced yet, it makes a rough estimate from volume and surface area. The formula assumes a wall of uniform thickness over the whole surface and the rest filled at the infill percentage, so it will not match the slicer.

Optionally add electricity, machine wear per hour, a failure allowance and labour, then a markup and a marketplace fee to get a selling price. Your settings are remembered in this browser, and the breakdown can be exported as CSV or xlsx.

How to use it

  1. Drop the G-code or .gcode.3mf exported from your slicer. Weight and print time are filled in for you. Without a file, just type the weight.
  2. Pick the material, then adjust density and price per kilogram to match your spool.
  3. Open any of the optional sections (electricity, machine wear, failure allowance, labour, selling price) and enter values. Empty fields are left out of the calculation.
  4. Read the breakdown and total in the table below, and save it as CSV or xlsx if you need it.

Limits and caveats

  • Weight from a 3D model is a rough pre-slice estimate. It ignores supports, brim, purge and top/bottom layers, so use sliced G-code for a real quote.
  • A model with holes or inconsistent face directions has no reliable volume, so no weight is estimated for it. Check it in the model inspector.
  • Densities and prices are typical values provided as defaults. They vary by brand and colour, so correct them from your filament's data sheet.
  • The currency only changes the symbol and the number of decimals. Amounts are not converted at an exchange rate.
  • Electricity is simply average power × print time. Print time is the slicer's estimate and real prints differ. Taxes and shipping are not included.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the STL estimate differ from my slicer's weight?

The STL estimate only looks at the model's volume and surface area. A real print depends on wall count, top and bottom layers, infill pattern, supports and brim, which are only known after slicing. For an accurate figure, drop the sliced G-code.

How is the selling price calculated?

The markup (%) is added to the total cost, and the result is divided so that this amount is still left after the marketplace takes its share of the selling price: price = total cost × (1 + markup) ÷ (1 − fee).

What does the failure allowance apply to?

It is a percentage of filament, electricity and machine wear together. If roughly one print in ten fails, enter 10%. Labour is not included in it.

Where are my settings stored?

Only in this browser's local storage; nothing is sent to a server. “Reset settings” clears them.
