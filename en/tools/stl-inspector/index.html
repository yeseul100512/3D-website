Problems found

  • Open edges (holes)

    The surface is not closed here, so the slicer has to guess where the solid ends. Small gaps are usually closed automatically; large holes can cause missing layers, or get filled where you wanted an opening.

  • Non-manifold edges

    Three or more faces meet at one edge. Inside and outside are ambiguous there, and slicers may drop or add material.

  • Flipped faces

    These faces point inward while their neighbours point outward. Slicers can treat the area as a hole or as inside-out material.

  • Zero-area triangles

    Triangles with no area (a line or a point). They add nothing to the shape and can confuse repair algorithms.

  • Duplicate faces

    The same triangle is stored more than once. The surface has a double skin at that spot.

  • Unreadable triangles (NaN / Inf)

    These triangles had coordinates that are not numbers and were left out when loading, so there may be holes where they were.

  • File cut off

    The file ends before all the triangles it announces. The download or export was probably interrupted; only the intact part is shown. Get the file again if you can.

Thin walls (estimate)

Casts a ray into the part from 20,000 points spread over the surface and measures the distance to the opposite wall. Walls thinner than two nozzle widths often print weak or not at all. This is an estimate, not an exact measurement.

    What this tool does

    This tool reads STL, 3MF and OBJ files and reports their size in millimetres, triangle count, surface area, volume and number of separate shells. It checks whether the part fits your printer's build volume as it is, only when laid on another side, or by how much it is too big, and warns when the size looks like it was saved in inches or metres.

    It then looks for the problems that make slicers fail or guess: open edges grouped into holes, edges shared by more than two faces, faces pointing the wrong way, zero-area and duplicate triangles, unreadable coordinates and files that were cut off. Each one comes with a count, a short explanation of what it means for printing, and a button that highlights exactly those places in the 3D view and zooms to them.

    Repairs are optional and separate: merge near-identical vertices, remove broken triangles, make faces point outward, and close holes one by one so that intended openings stay open. After a repair the check runs again and shows before and after counts, plus the bounding box compared to 0.001 mm. The result can be saved as STL, 3MF or OBJ, which also makes this a format converter, and the table of all loaded files can be saved as Excel or CSV.

    How to use it

    1. Drop one or more STL, 3MF or OBJ files on the box, or click it to choose them.
    2. Read the verdict and the size. If the size looks wrong by a factor of 25.4 or 1000, open “Change units” and rescale with one click.
    3. Choose your printer, or enter a custom build volume, to see whether the part fits.
    4. In “Problems found”, press Show next to a problem to highlight it and zoom to it in the 3D view.
    5. To repair, tick only the fixes you want and the holes that should be closed, then press Apply. Compare the before and after table; Undo brings back the file as loaded.
    6. Save the mesh as STL, 3MF or OBJ, or save the table of all files as Excel or CSV.

    Limits and caveats

    • It cannot fix self-intersections (parts of the surface passing through each other), walls with no thickness, or a model that was designed wrong. Those have to be fixed in the CAD or sculpting program.
    • Holes are closed with a flat patch made only from the hole's own edge points. That works for small gaps and flat openings; a hole in a curved surface gets a flat lid, not the missing curve. Holes with more than 2,000 edges and outlines that do not form a closed loop are reported but not closed.
    • Merging vertices can move a vertex by up to the tolerance, and triangles smaller than the tolerance collapse, so keep the tolerance small (default 0.001 mm).
    • The thin-wall figure is an estimate from 20,000 sample points, not a measurement of every wall. Thin spots between sample points can be missed, and the tips of sharp, wedge-shaped edges always count as thin.
    • Volume is only reliable when the mesh is watertight; otherwise it is marked as not reliable.
    • Bed fit compares the bounding box in axis-aligned orientations only. It does not try diagonal placement and ignores brims, skirts and purge areas.
    • Above about 5 million triangles the 3D view is switched off and only the numbers are shown; the thin-wall check is skipped above about 4 million triangles. How large a file opens at all depends on your device's memory.
    • 3MF: only the geometry is read. Colours, materials, print settings and plate layout are not kept when you save, and all objects in the file are checked together as one mesh.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is my file uploaded to a server?

    No. The file is read and processed only inside your browser and is not sent anywhere. There is no queue and no time limit.

    Why not just use the slicer's automatic repair?

    Automatic repair fixes everything at once and does not say what it changed, so intended openings can get filled and shapes can change without notice. Here you first see where each problem is, then choose the fixes yourself, and afterwards get the before and after counts and a bounding-box comparison.

    What does “non-manifold” mean?

    An edge shared by three or more faces, such as a fin attached to an edge or two bodies joined along a single edge. A real solid has exactly two faces on every edge, so the slicer cannot tell inside from outside at that spot.

    My model shows open edges but prints fine. Is that a problem?

    Often not. Slicers close small gaps on their own, which is why minor issues get the verdict “a slicer will probably fix these”. Large holes and non-manifold edges are what cause missing layers or filled openings, so check the sliced preview at those spots.

    Can it convert STL to 3MF or OBJ?

    Yes. Load the file and pick the other format under “Save mesh”. No repair has to be applied. Units are millimetres.

    The size is 25.4 times (or 1,000 times) too small. Why?

    STL files carry no unit. A file exported in inches or metres is read as millimetres and comes out too small. Use “Change units” to multiply by 25.4 or 1,000, then save it again.

    How big a file can it open?

    A 150 MB binary STL (3 million triangles) is checked in a few seconds on an ordinary laptop, with the 3D view. A 500 MB file (10 million triangles) can be checked on a desktop with enough memory, but only with numbers, without the 3D view.
