      What this tool does

      Based on what the part is for, which way it is loaded, the material and your priority, the tool recommends one Bambu Studio sparse infill pattern plus an alternative, a sparse infill density, wall loops and top/bottom shell layers. Every value comes with its reason and with what to change first if the print does not turn out well.

      Pattern characteristics follow the Bambu Lab wiki page on fill patterns. The density, wall and layer numbers are widely used starting points for a 0.4 mm nozzle and 0.2 mm layers. Pattern and setting names are the ones shown in the Bambu Studio interface.

      How to use it

      1. Choose what the part is for. Display piece, household item or load-bearing part: this sets the range for density and walls.
      2. Choose the direction and kind of load, relative to how the part sits on the build plate. If you are not sure, pick "several directions".
      3. Choose the material, your priority and the size, and tick the box if the top surface must look smooth. The recommendation updates with every change.
      4. Enter the values under Process → Strength in Bambu Studio. "Copy result" copies everything, reasons and cautions included, as plain text.

      Limits and caveats

      • These are rules of thumb for Bambu Studio. Nothing here calculates or guarantees strength, and we give no "X % stronger" figures because we have no data to back them. If an exact comparison matters, print test pieces under the same conditions and test them.
      • Strength usually depends more on wall loops and print orientation than on infill density. If the load pulls the layers apart, no infill setting will fix it: change the orientation first.
      • The density, wall and layer numbers are not from the Bambu wiki; they are common FDM starting points for a 0.4 mm nozzle and 0.2 mm layers. With another layer height, aim for a top thickness of roughly 0.8–1.2 mm instead of a layer count.
      • Pattern characteristics come from the Bambu Lab wiki page Fill Pattern Introduction (also read in its Chinese edition). Which pattern suits which use is our reading of that page, not an official Bambu Lab recommendation.
      • Pattern and setting names were checked against Bambu Studio 02.08.02.61. Names can differ between versions. Material notes cover PLA, ABS/ASA and carbon-fibre filaments only, and other slicers are not covered.

      Frequently asked questions

      Does a higher infill density make the part stronger?

      Somewhat, but for the same amount of filament, adding wall loops first usually does more. That is why this tool starts density at the low end of its range and tells you to add walls first when the part is too weak.

      What if I want 100 % infill?

      According to the Bambu wiki, only six patterns support 100 % density: Concentric, Rectilinear, Aligned Rectilinear, Hilbert Curve, Archimedean Chords and Octagram Spiral. In practice, a large number of wall loops is usually the better choice.

      The recommended pattern is not in my Bambu Studio list.

      2D Lattice and Locked Zag were added in fairly recent versions. Update Bambu Studio, or use the alternative pattern shown with the result.

      Which pattern is the strongest?

      It depends on the load. Tri-hexagon suits side shear, 2D Lattice with a small angle suits compression from above, and Gyroid or Cubic suit loads from several directions. We do not give percentage differences between patterns because we have no data for them.
